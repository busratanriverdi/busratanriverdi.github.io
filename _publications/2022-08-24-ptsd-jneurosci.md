abstract: >
    This paper explores the interaction between hippocampal fear reactivity and physiological fear responses in predicting PTSD symptom severity in trauma-exposed individuals. We found that individuals who showed heightened activity in the hippocampus—along with stronger stress-related physiological responses—also reported more severe PTSD symptoms. These findings suggest that disrupted hippocampal responses may serve as a biomarker for PTSD vulnerability, particularly in explaining the failure to distinguish between safety and threat. This impairment may lead individuals to generalize fear across safe and unsafe situations, increasing risk for chronic distress and maladaptive behavior.
